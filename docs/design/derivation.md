# Derivation: raw observations → path model

- requirements: REQ-DRV-001 … REQ-DRV-015, REQ-FMT-020, REQ-FMT-021
- adr: 0008, 0012, 0014
- research: R-0001, R-0002, R-0004, R-0005

`pathtrace derive` (and `probe`, after merge) turns the raw layer into the derived layer: PATH, SERS, PLCY, VALD. Derivation is a deterministic, pure function of the raw sections and the tool version (REQ-DRV-001). It never changes raw sections, so it can be re-run on any trace whenever a method improves. Every derived field records `method` (`name/N`), `confidence` in [0, 1], `status` (`known`, `estimated`, `unknown`) and the raw source it used.

Methods are versioned independently. Changing a method's output semantics bumps its `/N` (REQ-DRV-012).

## Time base

| Step | Method | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Endpoint REALTIME → MONO_RAW | `clock.pairs-piecewise/4` | Clock pairs (≈1 Hz, ~30 ns width) in mono order. A jump in (real − mono) > 1 ms between neighbouring pairs is a clock step and is recorded in PATH.clock.\<ep\>.steps. The map is cut at every step and every suspend (below) into step-free segments; within a segment it is piecewise-linear interpolation between consecutive pairs, extrapolated with the nearest pair interval's slope. Where a stamp could lie in more than one segment, a mono hint picks it (Clock steps, below). Across a suspend the map is a step instead of a line, and the suspend is recorded in PATH.clock.\<ep\>.suspends |
| b.mono → a.mono | `clock.exchange-minfilter/4` | Fitted on suspend-continuous clocks (below). For each exchange, offset θ = ((t2−t1)+(t3−t4))/2 and delay δ = (t4−t1)−(t3−t2). Offset: keep the 25% lowest-δ exchanges; θ0 is their mean θ at t_ref, their mean t1. Skew: lower-envelope fit, else the clock pairs, else 0 (below). The offset is θ0 + skew·(t1−t_ref). Uncertainty = min δ / 2 (draft spec §8). This assumes symmetric paths (`split_method = symmetric_assumed`) |
| Trace time | — | ns since `META.session.a.t0_mono` on a.mono |

### Clock skew (`clock.exchange-minfilter/2`)

- **Problem.** `/1` fitted the skew by least squares through the 25% lowest-δ exchanges. On a short, high-variance path (a relayed overlay, RTT 0.25–39 s) that leaves a few samples whose θ error is the path's delay asymmetry, so the slope is noise: field traces gave −1 655 to +1 813 ppm where the endpoints' NTP-disciplined clock pairs give ≈16 ppm (issue #105). At 1.8 ‰ a 34 s session carries about 60 ms of drift error into every b-side time.
- **Constraints.** Pure function of the raw layer; REQ-DRV-002 asks for a lower-envelope skew fit; queueing only ever adds delay, so the minimum of each one-way delay is the clean signal; the pairs are a second, independent observation of the same rates but depend on NTP being in step at both ends.
- **Alternatives.** Clamping the least-squares slope keeps the noise up to the bound. Using only the pairs makes the result depend on NTP at both ends and gives nothing on an undisciplined clock. A robust regression over the θ samples (Theil–Sen) still reads delay asymmetry as skew.
- **Choice.** Two envelopes, over every exchange with δ ≥ 0: forward d_f = t2 − t1 against t1, backward d_b = t4 − t3 against t4. Each has the line under all its points that minimises the summed vertical distance to them (the linear-programming fit of Moon, Skelly and Towsley), which is the edge of the points' lower convex hull spanning their mean x. Forward delay is θ(t) + owd_f, backward owd_b − θ(t), so their slopes s_f and s_b give skew = (s_f − s_b)/2 and `skew_exchange_uncertainty_ppb` = |s_f + s_b|/2 (the two disagree only through delay changes; it qualifies `skew_exchange_ppb`, not `skew_ppb`, and is recorded whichever source is used). The envelope is used (`skew_source: "exchange"`) when the exchanges span at least 10 s and both |s_f| and |s_b| are at most 500 ppm, the largest frequency correction the kernel NTP discipline accepts (`MAXFREQ`, RFC 5905's 500 PPM tolerance), so a larger slope is the path's delay changing, not a clock. Otherwise the skew comes from the pairs (`skew_source: "pairs"`) when each endpoint's step-free pairs span at least 10 s: per endpoint, rate = Σ Δmono / Σ Δreal over consecutive pairs that are not a clock step, and skew = rate_b / rate_a − 1, which assumes both REALTIME clocks run at the same rate, as NTP-disciplined clocks do; this too must be within 500 ppm. Otherwise skew = 0 (`skew_source: "none"`). The candidates are recorded whichever is used: `skew_exchange_ppb` and `skew_pairs_ppb` (null where not computable), with `span_ns`, the exchanges' t1 span.
- **Confidence.** 0.4 when fewer than 3 exchanges are kept for the offset or the skew source is `none`; 0.6 from `pairs`; from `exchange`, 0.9 when at least 8 are kept and the span is at least 60 s, else 0.7.
- **Rationale.** The envelope reads only the uncongested floor of each direction, so queueing does not move it, and the two directions check each other. The bound and the fallback stop what remains (a floor that moves, as when a relay changes) from becoming a skew no clock has. `/1` traces are re-derivable: the raw exchanges and pairs are unchanged.
- **Validated by.** Unit tests in `crates/pt-derive/src/clock.rs`: a known skew recovered under heavy one-sided queueing, a short high-variance session falling back to the pairs, the pairs skew composed from two endpoints' clock pairs by `Offset::from_raw`, and an out-of-bound envelope rejected.

### Suspends (`clock.exchange-minfilter/4`, `clock.pairs-piecewise/3`)

- **Problem.** MONO_RAW stops while its host is suspended and REALTIME and the peer's MONO_RAW do not (probe.md §4.3). `/2` and `pairs-piecewise/1` fitted one line through each mapping, so a suspend became skew or interpolation: in a field trace whose initiator slept 936 s between two clock pairs (1765 s in all, issue #111) every b-side time was moved by a share of the suspended time, giving one-way delays and queue delays of hundreds of seconds and negative RTTs.
- **Constraints.** Pure function of the raw layer; a suspend is identified by the clock that counts it, not by REALTIME, which NTP also steps; traces whose pairs have no `boot` derive exactly as before; the trace time axis stays a.mono, on which a suspended interval of a has no length.
- **Alternatives.** Splitting the session at each suspend into independently fitted pieces leaves the pieces without the exchanges that span them, and a piece too short for a skew. Reading the suspend from REALTIME − MONO_RAW also takes NTP steps for suspends. Taking the `stall {cause: "suspend"}` events alone as the suspends ties derivation to an event an older or other prober may not write, and an event lost to a full logger channel (probe.md §1) would lose its suspend; so the pairs find each suspend and the event only narrows where it is placed (#126). Marking the transmit times inside a pair interval unknown instead of collapsing them drops every packet of that interval from the delay estimates, where the event leaves only those of one pacer loop misplaced; an unknown receive time would count as a loss, so it could not be applied to receive times at all.
- **Choice.** Between consecutive pairs of an endpoint with `boot`, a growth of boot − mono by more than 2 ms (the prober's `STALL_NS`) is a suspend of that length. Mono stands still while the host sleeps, so the suspend has one mono, the wake's, and every time the endpoint records after the wake is at or above it. It is placed at the earliest of two such times: the `t` of the endpoint's earliest `stall {cause: "suspend"}` event with `t` in `(earlier pair, later pair]` (the pacer records it at its first loop after the wake, probe.md §4.3), else the mono of the later pair. An event in the interval can be another suspend's, so two kinds are passed over: one whose `suspended_ns` exceeds the interval's growth by more than max(2 ms, 10%) (BOOTTIME is slewed against MONO_RAW between the pacer's reads and the pairs'), and, where the previous interval's suspend was placed at its later pair, the first event of this interval when its `suspended_ns` is within that tolerance of the previous suspend's length or is not recorded: that suspend woke before its later pair and the pacer recorded it after (#131). Passing over this interval's own event only falls back to the later pair, which is never before the wake. It is recorded as `PATH.clock.<ep>.suspends [{mono, since_mono, suspended_ns, placed_mono, placed_by}]` (`mono` the later pair's mono, `since_mono` the earlier pair's, so the suspend lies somewhere in `(since_mono, mono)`; `placed_mono` where it is placed, and `placed_by` `"stall_event"` where an event placed it before the later pair, else `"pair"`). Two suspends in one pair interval are one suspend of their summed length, placed at the first event. The suspend-continuous clock of an endpoint is mono plus every suspended time placed at or before it. The offset fit runs on the exchanges with t1 and t4 on a's continuous clock and t2 and t3 on b's, unchanged otherwise. An exchange with a time strictly between the earlier pair and the placement of a suspend is left out (a time at or after the placement was recorded after the wake), because which side of the suspend that time lies on is unknown and its θ could be off by the whole suspend; one that straddles a suspend has a δ that includes it and is never among the lowest 25%. b → a maps b.mono to b's continuous clock, through the fitted offset, and back to a.mono; a time that falls inside one of a's suspends maps to the a.mono at which it is placed. `pairs-piecewise/3` maps a REALTIME stamp between the two pairs of a suspend with the earlier pair's offset while that gives a mono below the placement, and otherwise with the later pair's offset, but never below the placement, so both mappings put the suspend at the same mono and stay monotonic. Without a suspend both methods give `/1`'s (`pairs-piecewise`) and `/2`'s (`exchange-minfilter`) results; with suspends that no event narrows, `pairs-piecewise/2`'s and `exchange-minfilter/3`'s, which placed every suspend at the later pair.
- **Rationale.** The continuous clocks of the two endpoints run at nearly the same rate across a suspend, so one line fits them again, and the existing skew rules apply unchanged. The placement is never before the wake, and is after it by the time from the wake to the pacer's first loop after it, which runs by the pacer's next send deadline (50 ms when it has none); without the event, by up to one pair interval (≈1 s). A peer time that is truly in that gap is collapsed onto the placement, an error bounded by the gap rather than by the suspended time. Placed at the later pair, the packets the peer sent in the gap showed a one-way delay near 0: in a field trace with a 1.5 s initiator suspend (2026-10-19, #126) the b → a `base_delay` window over it was 0.0 ms where the others were 94-111 ms.
- **Validated by.** Unit tests in `crates/pt-derive/src/clock.rs`: an initiator suspend and a responder suspend each recover the offset and skew of a session without one, a time inside the suspend collapses onto its placement, REALTIME stamps around a suspend map monotonically and with the earlier offset before it, a `stall {cause: "suspend"}` event in the pair interval moves the placement, the map and the left-out exchanges to it (and an event outside the interval, or of the other endpoint, does not), and a previous suspend's event recorded after its later pair, or an event longer than the interval's growth, does not place the next suspend; and the re-derivation of the 2026-10-19 field traces (issue #111).

### Clock steps (`clock.pairs-piecewise/4`)

- **Problem.** `/3` sorted an endpoint's clock pairs by REALTIME and interpolated between neighbours (issue #128). When REALTIME steps backwards (an NTP or chrony step), the pairs before and after the step have overlapping real values, so sorted by real they interleave: `map` interpolated between pairs from both sides of the step, and a stamp in the overlap took a mono from the wrong side, off by up to the whole step. A forward step was recorded in `steps` and skipped by the pairs rate, but `map` still drew one line across it, so every stamp in that pair interval was moved by a share of the step.
- **Constraints.** Pure function of the raw layer; MONO_RAW never steps, so mono order is the order in which the pairs were taken; a REALTIME stamp in the overlap of a backward step happened twice on the real axis, and the stamp alone cannot say which time it was; a trace without a step must derive exactly as before; an unknown time is recorded as unknown, never guessed.
- **Alternatives.** Dropping the pairs on one side of the step leaves its stamps without a map. Marking every stamp in an overlap unknown loses the receive time of every packet in it, which then counts as a loss. Choosing the later segment always is wrong for every stamp taken before the step. Inferring the step's place from the stamps' own order (`rx` rows are not stored in receive order after `merge`) has nothing to stand on.
- **Choice.** The pairs are kept in mono order (repeated monos dropped), and the map is cut into step-free segments at each clock step and each suspend. Each segment maps a real stamp by interpolating between its own pairs, extrapolated with its end pairs' slope into the cut beside it. A segment's mapping is a candidate only where its mono lies on that segment's side of the cut: for a suspend, below or at or above its placement; for a step, whose place in the pair interval is unknown, below the next segment's first pair or above the previous segment's last pair. One candidate is the mono. Where several are (the overlap of a backward step, or a forward step smaller than the pair interval), the caller's **hint** decides: a mono the stamp is not expected to precede, given in trace time: for a transmit stamp the packet's intended send time (`tx_intended` plus `tx_user_lag`), for a receive stamp the packet's transmit time, for an echo the probe's transmit time. The earliest candidate at or after hint − 1 ms (the step threshold, so an offset error below it never flips the choice) is taken, else the latest one. Without a hint several candidates give no mono (unknown). Where no segment gives a candidate (a real stamp in the range a forward step skipped), the stamp maps into the cut: to a suspend's placement, or by linear interpolation between the cut's two pairs, `/3`'s result. Steps are recorded as before, now in mono order (`{mono, jump_ns}`, `mono` the later pair's), and `PATH.clock.<ep>.segments` (new key) counts the segments (0 without pairs).
- **Skew.** The skew is not fitted per segment. A REALTIME step or slew does not move MONO_RAW, and the offset is fitted between the two endpoints' MONO_RAW clocks (`clock.exchange-minfilter/4`), so it needs no cut; splitting it per segment would leave each piece without the exchanges that span a step, as for suspends. The pairs skew (`skew_source: "pairs"`) already sums only step-free pair intervals; a REALTIME slew after a step still enters it as the REALTIME rate it assumes equal at both ends, which its 500 ppm bound and its fallback rank (below the envelope) already allow for. `clock.exchange-minfilter` stays `/4`.
- **Rationale.** The receive time of a packet follows its transmit time by its one-way delay, and a transmit stamp follows its intended send time by the sender's lag, so "the earliest candidate not before the hint" picks the right side of a step of |J| whenever the stamp lags its hint by less than |J| − 1 ms. Two candidates of one stamp lie |J| apart, and the rule fails only when the wrong one is the earlier and still at or after hint − 1 ms: a receive stamp taken after a backward step (in its overlap), or taken before a forward step within the step's pair interval, whose one-way delay is at least |J| − 1 ms. It then maps |J| early, silently, not to unknown: the hint is a lower bound with no upper bound, so the map cannot tell that stamp from one truly at the earlier candidate. Transmit stamps lag their hint by the sender's lag (microseconds), so they are safe for any step above the threshold. For receive stamps the condition holds for chrony's default `makestep` (1 s) on every path this tool measures, but not for an `ntpd` step near its 128 ms threshold over a path whose one-way delay (a GEO hop, or a standing queue of 150-500 ms under load) reaches it: there the receive stamps of the affected interval (the overlap, at most |J| of mono, or the part of one pair interval before a forward step) map |J| early, and their one-way delays read |J| low. Without a step the map is the single segment `/3` drew, so step-free traces derive unchanged, and `/3` traces are re-derivable: the raw pairs and stamps are unchanged.
- **Validated by.** Unit tests in `crates/pt-derive/src/clock.rs`: a backward step's overlap maps each stamp to the side its hint names (and to no mono without a hint), a forward step is not interpolated across, steps are found in mono order with interleaving reals, and the existing suspend tests unchanged.

Packet transmit time is SND when present (closest to the wire), else SCHED, else user-space (`tx_intended + tx_user_lag`). The choice is counted per direction in `PATH.<dir>.timestamps`. A trace from a macOS initiator, which has no kernel TX stamps, counts every a→b packet as `user` (probe.md §9.2). Receive stamps are used as recorded, whatever their source (`pkt.rx_src`: a kernel stamp, or a user-space time where the socket gave none). They are counted rather than separated: `PATH.<dir>.timestamps.rx_counts {kernel, user, unknown}` over every received packet, and the same map as `rx_src` on `jitter` (the samples it used) and `queue` (the saturating flows' arrivals), so a reader can tell whether an estimate rests on mixed stamps (`unknown`: no `rx_src` recorded).

## Host drops

| Step | Method | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Receiver host drops | `hostdrop.rxq-gap/1` | For each receiving socket, take its arrivals (all flows of the direction, by receive time). Where `rxq_drops` increases between two consecutive arrivals, the socket dropped datagrams in that gap. A lost packet to that socket whose transmit time lies between the transmit times of those two arrivals is a host-drop candidate. Each gap marks at most as many candidates as the socket dropped in it (`rxq_drops` increase), earliest transmit time first, as **host drops**, so `host_drops.packets` never exceeds `socket_drops`. When a gap has more candidates than drops, which of them the socket dropped is unknown; the unmarked ones stay losses, and the gap and its excess candidates are counted (`ambiguous_gaps`, `ambiguous_losses`). Host drops are excluded from the loss-based estimates that say so below (`capacity.saturated-arrivals/2`, `queue.loss-onset/3`, `policer.lossy-flat/2`, `loss.gilbert-runs/2`), counted in `PATH.<dir>.host_drops {packets, gaps, socket_drops, ambiguous_gaps, ambiguous_losses}`, and treated there as unknown (neither delivered nor lost). Other methods still count them as not received: the light-train counts of `capacity.train-dispersion/1`, `mtu.ladder/2` and `timeline.outage/1`. The receiving socket of a lost packet is the one most arrivals of its flow used (the main socket if none arrived). Traces without `rxq_drops` (PTF 1.0) have no host drops by this rule, and `host_drops.status` is `unknown` |
| Receiver host drops without `rxq_drops` | `hostdrop.host-fullsock/1` | Applies where the receiver recorded no `rxq_drops` but its `netstack` table has `udp_fullsock` (PTF 1.2, macOS, probe.md §9.6). Over the receiver's non-null `udp_fullsock` rows in time order, with no `iface_change` segmentation (the host-wide counter does not restart when the sampler follows another interface): a decrease between consecutive rows (a counter wrap or a reboot) gives `unknown` with `reason: "host-wide counter decreased"`; otherwise last minus first is the rise. No rise means no UDP socket on the host was dropped into, so the probe's sockets dropped nothing: `known`, `packets: 0`, `source: ["netstack"]`, and no packet is marked. A rise gives `unknown` with `reason: "host-wide counter rose"` and the rise as `host_socket_drops`, because which socket dropped is unknown. Without `udp_fullsock` the field is exactly `hostdrop.rxq-gap/1`'s, its `unknown` reason text (`no rxq_drops in this trace (PTF 1.0)`) included |

Sender-side drops need no rule: a datagram the sender failed to send has no `tx` row (probe.md §4.1). Drops in the sender's own egress qdisc are a property of the access link as the host uses it; the `qdisc` table records them alongside, and `PATH.<dir>.local_qdisc_drops {drops, segments}` (`qdisc.drop-count/2`) reports the sender's root-qdisc drops during the session. The `drops` column is cumulative per interface and restarts at each of the sender's `iface_change` events (probe.md §4.4), and at a qdisc replacement, where it decreases. So the rows are cut into segments at each `iface_change` and at each decrease, and `drops` is the sum over segments of last minus first. `segments` counts them. Drops between a segment's last row and the switch are not observed. (`/1` took last minus first over the whole table, which spans two interfaces after a switch.) A sender with no `qdisc` table (PTF 1.0, or macOS, which has no qdisc, probe.md §9.6) gets `unknown` with `reason: "no qdisc counters recorded"`; a macOS trace never reads `udps_fullsock` or `ifi_oerrors` here. An unknown field carries no counts, and an unknown count never enters an estimate as 0.

## Per-direction fields

| Field | Method | Rule (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| `base_delay` | `delay.light-window-min/1` | One-way delay (OWD) of the light flow. Base = minimum over ±5 s windows, emitted as 1 s segments in SERS `base_delay[dir]`. Scalar = median of segments |
| `path_change` | `pathchange.base-delay-step/1` | Step changes of the base delay, i.e. a path that changed mid-session (relay to direct, route or interface switch, a suspend), recorded by effect (ADR 0015, PTF 1.3). Uses the `base_delay[dir]` segments outside loaded intervals (any saturating or chirp flow in either direction), so a standing queue is not read as a step. At each segment k with 10 usable segments on each side, the step is the difference between the medians of the 10 before and the 10 after. It is a change when it exceeds max(10 ms, 5 × the median absolute difference between consecutive segments). The threshold is absolute because one-way delay carries the unknown clock offset. Candidates are ranked by the step of the means, which peaks at the step where the medians plateau, and taken greedily, at least 10 segments apart. `changes` lists `{t, before_ns, after_ns}` in time order (t is within about 5 s of the step, the base window's half-width), `count` their number. `unknown` with fewer than 20 usable segments |
| `jitter` | `jitter.light-residual/1` | OWD − base for light-flow packets in **quiet** periods: the initiator's P2 and P10 phases (from EVNT `phase_start`), excluding intervals with any saturating or chirp flow in either direction. The phase timeline matters because TCP reference flows also load the path but are not in `pkt`. Series plus quantiles (p1…p99) plus autocorrelation at lags 1–10 |
| `size_delay` | `delay.size-slope/1` | Median OWD of quiet-period train packets (MTU) minus light packets (128 B) gives a per-byte serialisation delay estimate (R-0002 comp(s)) |
| `capacity` | `capacity.saturated-arrivals/3` | Saturated windows are where the heavy flow's queueing delay exceeds `max(2 ms, 25% of the controller target)`, or where heavy-flow packets are being lost (host drops excluded; /1 counted them): loss also proves that demand exceeded capacity, and a policer builds no queue (REQ-PRB-013). Every arrival in that direction during those windows becomes a delivery opportunity (t, bytes) in SERS `capacity_opps[dir]` (`quantum_bytes` is the per-row size, R-0001). 100 ms rate bins go to `capacity_rate[dir]`. Segments, in time order: `saturated` with workload `heavy`; `estimated` with workload `chirp` from `capacity.chirp-dispersion/1` (below), each with `method`, `rate_bps` and `chirps`; `unknown` elsewhere (`outside_segments`). `estimated_rate_bps` is the median of the chirp segments' rates (null without one). Without saturated windows but with a chirp estimate the field is `estimated` (confidence 0.4, `representation: "segments"`, no `median_rate_bps`); with neither it is `unknown`. `/2` emitted only `saturated` segments |
| `capacity` (chirps) | `capacity.chirp-dispersion/1` | Per P3 chirp (flow kind `chirp`, one `train`, probe.md §6), all packets received with transmit and receive times and none a host drop, outside every saturated window. Once a chirp's gaps are shorter than one packet time at the bottleneck its packets queue and leave the bottleneck one packet time apart, so their arrival gaps exceed their transmit gaps. The chirp's rate is the bytes of its trailing run of such expanded gaps over that run's arrival span; no rate when the run has fewer than 3 gaps (the chirp never queued: capacity above its top rate, about 1.27 Gbit/s at 1500 B). Chirps whose first transmissions are at most 2 s apart form one segment, from the first one's first transmission to the last one's last arrival, at the median of their rates. Behind a token-bucket policer or frame-aggregating Wi-Fi a 10 ms chirp passes at the burst rate (probe.md §7), so the estimate can exceed the sustained rate; that is why it stays `estimated` and never enters `capacity_opps` or the Mahimahi export |
| `capacity_light` | `capacity.train-dispersion/1` | Per light train: arrival offsets relative to the first packet (SERS `train_offsets[dir]`), dispersion rate (n−1)·size·8/(t_last−t_first), losses, and the workload context (quiet or during saturation) (R-0002, ADR 0010) |
| `queue` | `queue.loss-onset/3` | Per saturation: depth_time = the maximum heavy-flow queueing delay in the 500 ms before each loss (the full-buffer delay for tail drop), median across losses, taken over the flows whose losses revealed the buffer; the overall max if no flow lost packets (a lower bound). depth_bytes = depth_time × saturated rate. Drain cross-check (`drain`): for each `heavy` flow whose last received packet still found a queue above 2.5 ms (the sender stopped into a standing queue), that queueing delay is the drain time and the bytes the direction delivered between the moment that packet would have arrived over an empty queue and its arrival are the queue it found; `drain {flows, time_ns, bytes, rate_bps, bytes_ratio}` holds their medians, bytes × 8 / time, and bytes over `depth_bytes` (null where not computable). It is recorded beside the depth, not folded into it: a ratio far from 1 says the loss-onset depth and the drain disagree. Discipline: `policer` when at least half the lossy saturating flows lost packets with queueing delay < 2 ms; else `aqm_suspected` when a saturating flow saw CE marks; else, over the `heavy` flows that lost packets (the flows that overdrive and so probe the buffer, probe.md §7), `droptail` when their median onset ratio (onset delay over the flow's max) is ≥ 0.8, `aqm_suspected` when it is lower; `unknown` when no heavy flow lost packets. The verdict's inputs are written beside it: `onset_ratio` (that median, null without a lossy heavy flow), `onset_threshold` (0.8), `lossy_flows`, `lossy_heavy_flows` and `low_queue_flows` |
| `policer` | `policer.lossy-flat/3` | When the discipline is `policer`: `rate_bps` = the median saturated 100 ms bin (the delivery rate while the policer drops); `burst_bytes` = the median, over the lossy saturating flows, of the token bucket each shows before its first loss: the most bytes that flow got delivered above `rate_bps` over any interval ending at its first loss (host drops are not losses), counted by transmit time, since a policer queues nothing; `burst_flows` = how many flows gave one. `scope: "direction"`: the verdict covers the direction; each class has its own in PLCY (`policer.class-lossy-flat/1`). `/2` wrote `burst_bytes: null` |
| PLCY `classes.<class>.policer` | `policer.class-lossy-flat/1` | The `policer.lossy-flat/3` rule applied to one P7 class saturation (flow kind `class-sat`, a→b: `udp/high-port`, `udp/443`, `udp/443-quic`), recorded on that UDP class's entry with `scope: "class"`. `losses`: its lost packets (host drops are not losses). `depth_time_ns`: the median, over those losses, of the maximum queueing delay in the 500 ms before each (as `queue.loss-onset/3` takes it per flow). Detected (`estimated`, confidence 0.6, `detected: true`) when it lost packets at `depth_time_ns` < 2 ms, a policer queueing nothing; then `rate_bps` = the delivery rate in the lossy period, over the arrivals of its packets sent at or after its first loss, the bytes of all but the first over the span from the first to the last (null with fewer than 2 such arrivals), and `burst_bytes` = the most bytes it got delivered above `rate_bps` over any interval ending at its first loss, counted by transmit time (null without a rate). Not detected (`unknown`, confidence 0, `detected: false`) when it lost nothing or lost at a queue of 2 ms or more. `unknown` with `detected: null` and a `reason` when the class had no saturation (P7 skipped it or the trace has none) or no loss had a queueing delay (no base delay) |
| `loss` | `loss.gilbert-runs/2` | Losses (host drops excluded; /1 counted them) of quiet-period light-flow, light-train and control datagrams, taken in transmit order (the path's Gilbert chain advances on every packet, so every packet the probe puts on the path must be observed: EXP-0002) → events (t, burst length) in SERS `loss_events[dir]`. Gilbert model p = P(good→bad), r = P(bad→good), and the stored `h` = 1, `k` = 0, which are **loss** probabilities: `h` = P(loss \| bad), `k` = P(loss \| good) (every packet in the bad state is lost, none in the good one). This is the reverse of netem's own h and k, which are delivery probabilities; netem's `gemodel p r 1-h 1-k` takes loss probabilities in its third and fourth places, so the stored `h` and `k` go there unchanged (`gemodel p r 100% 0%`, R-0005, ptf-format.md "Derived fields"). With Wilson 95% intervals (`p_ci95`, `r_ci95`) and the transition counts behind them (`good_n`, `good_to_bad`, `bad_n`, `bad_to_good`; p = good_to_bad / good_n, r = bad_to_good / bad_n). The counts let any reader compute an interval at another level, for example a family-wise level when several intervals are judged together (EXP-0002-r6). Added in 1.0.1; older traces lack them, and `derive` adds them |
| `reorder` | `reorder.late-arrivals/1` | Per flow, a packet is reordered if it arrives after a packet with a higher sequence number; rate plus displacement histogram |
| `duplicate` | `duplicate.count/1` | Rows in `dup` / received rows |
| `mtu` | `mtu.ladder/2` | Path MTU = largest ladder size received. Black hole = larger sizes lost with no ICMP Packet Too Big; ICMP MTU values are recorded. `blackhole` is null when the socket the sender sent the ladder from (FLOW `sock`) recorded `icmp_errors: false` in META (PTF 1.2, macOS, probe.md §9.5), because the absence of an ICMP error is then not evidence; `path_mtu` stays known. Otherwise as `/1` |
| `ecn` | `tos.light-cycle/1` | Per ECN codepoint sent on the light flow: `passed`, `bleached` (arrives as not-ECT), `remarked`, `marked` (CE seen) or `dropped` (loss rate ≥ 5× that of not-ECT) |
| `dscp` | `tos.light-cycle/1` | Per DSCP sent: `preserved` or `rewritten` to the observed value(s) |
| `summary` | `summary.pantheon5/1` | Rate (median saturated 100 ms bin), one-way base delay, intrinsic loss rate, buffer bytes, delivery process (`isochronous` if the inter-delivery CV in saturation is < 1, else `poisson`) (R-0004) |

## Exports (REQ-FMT-021)

Version 2 of `capacity.saturated-arrivals`, `queue.loss-onset`, `policer.lossy-flat` and `loss.gilbert-runs` differs from version 1 only by excluding host drops; on a trace without `rxq_drops` the results are identical.

`queue.loss-onset/3` differs from `/2` only in the discipline: `/2` took the onset ratio over every lossy saturating flow, the P7 class saturations (`class-sat`) included.
*Problem:* a class saturation lasts 3 s and so never overdrives (probe.md §7); it holds the controller's queue target, so its maximum queueing delay is that target, not the buffer, and any loss it sees happens at its maximum delay whatever the discipline. Under fq_codel (`aqm-ecn` ground truth) each class flow's ratio came out either about 1.0 or about 0.5–0.7 from run to run, at every commit sampled from 5e309dc to main; whenever all three came out near 1.0 they outvoted the two heavy flows (0.5–0.7 in every run) and the median gave `droptail` (EXP-0002-r15 and r16; issue #103). *Constraints:* `policer` must still see every lossy flow, since a policer drops the class flows at no queue too; the depth estimate and every other field stay as they are; derivation stays re-derivable from raw. *Alternatives rejected:* bisecting the capture changes for a regression, as no commit changes the class flows' loss pattern, the split shows at both ends of the range, and a run fails only when three coin flips land the same way; lowering the 0.8 threshold, which leaves the class flows voting on evidence they cannot carry; requiring a minimum loss count per flow, which the class flows meet in some failing runs (16 losses at ratio 1.0). *Choice:* the droptail/AQM ratio uses the heavy flows only, and with no lossy heavy flow the discipline is `unknown` (no flow probed the buffer). *Rationale:* only an overdriving flow fills a tail-drop buffer, so only its losses tell tail drop (losses at the maximum delay) from early drop. *Validated by:* `dir::tests::class_saturations_do_not_vote_on_the_discipline`, and re-deriving the r16 `aqm-ecn` trace (`aqm_suspected`) and the `rate-delay-asym` and `policer` traces (unchanged).

`capacity.saturated-arrivals/3`, `capacity.chirp-dispersion/1` and `policer.lossy-flat/3` (issue #110), and the keys added to `queue.loss-onset/3`, `timestamps.source-count/1`, `jitter.light-residual/1` and `vald.reference/1`.
*Problem:* a pre-capture audit found methods weaker than the requirements marked met against them. REQ-DRV-004 asks for chirp `estimated` segments, but no chirp estimate was ever made. REQ-DRV-006 asks for a drain-time cross-check, which did not exist, and the 0.8 onset ratio behind the droptail/AQM verdict was never written down, so a reader could not see how close a verdict was (#103). REQ-DRV-007 asks for a burst, but `burst_bytes` was always null. Kernel and user-space receive stamps were mixed without being counted, and VALD gave percentiles without sample counts or intervals.
*Constraints:* derivation stays pure and re-derivable, so old traces gain the fields on `derive`; a changed output means a method bump (REQ-DRV-012), but new keys on unchanged values do not; the Mahimahi export must still replay only measured saturation (`saturated` segments), never a chirp estimate.
*Alternatives rejected:* pathChirp's excursion analysis, which needs per-path tuning of excursion thresholds and reads available bandwidth, where the dispersion of the queued tail reads the bottleneck rate the `saturated` segments measure; train dispersion as the `estimated` source, since `capacity_light` already holds it as the light-workload view (ADR 0010), which shares its queue with the heavy flows, and the P3 chirps are the phase the plan sends for the capacity estimate (probe.md §6); folding the drain into the depth, which would hide the very disagreement the check exists to show; dropping user-space receive stamps, which would empty every b→a estimate of a macOS initiator, whose stamps are all user-space.
*Choice:* the rules in the table above.
*Rationale:* each addition records an observation or the verdict's input, never replaces one, so every field `/2` wrote keeps its meaning except where the version is bumped.
*Validated by:* `dir::tests::chirp_rate_recovers_the_bottleneck` (20, 100 and 400 Mbit/s FIFO bottlenecks within 1%, nothing above the chirp's top rate or with a lost packet, chirps grouped into one segment and none inside a saturated window), `dir::tests::policer_burst_is_the_bucket` (a 30 000 B bucket recovered within one packet), `dir::tests::drain_counts_bytes_inside_the_last_queue`, `dir::tests::onset_ratio_is_the_heavy_median` and `policy::tests::quantile_interval_by_order_statistics`. Ground-truth recovery of the chirp segments and the policer burst (REQ-DRV-013) is not yet run (no EXP run in this change).

`policer.class-lossy-flat/1` (issue #129), a new `policer` key on each PLCY UDP class entry.
*Problem:* REQ-DRV-007 asks for a policer detected per class, but `policer.lossy-flat/3` gives one verdict per direction, a vote over every lossy saturating flow, and PLCY recorded only a capacity ratio and a loss differential per class. A policer keyed on one port or protocol (tbf on one UDP port, simulator-requirements.md S2) then shows as a lower capacity ratio, never as a policer with a rate and a burst.
*Constraints:* the per-direction `policer` and `queue` fields keep their values and versions; derivation stays pure and re-derivable, so older traces gain the key on `derive`; a new key on unchanged values needs no `policy.matrix` bump (REQ-DRV-012); only P7 sends per-class saturations, so only the UDP classes have the evidence.
*Alternatives rejected:* a per-class map under `PATH.<dir>.policer`, which would put class treatment outside PLCY, where every other per-class fact lives and where the overlay flag already qualifies it; voting a class's verdict from the direction's flows, which would credit one class with another's policer; the direction's median saturated bin as the class rate, which is the heavy flows' rate and not the class's when only the class is policed.
*Choice:* the rule in the table above, per class saturation flow, computed beside the queue estimate of the direction a→b and recorded on the class entry with `scope: "class"`.
*Rationale:* a class saturation is one flow, so the direction's majority vote reduces to that flow's own losses at low delay; after its first loss the bucket is empty and the flow is delivered at the token rate, so the lossy period's delivery rate is the policer's rate, and the burst before the first loss is its bucket, as for the direction.
*Validated by:* `dir::tests::class_policer_reads_one_class_saturation` (a 10 Mbit/s, 30 000 B bucket recovered within 1% and one packet; tail drop at a full queue and a lossless saturation not detected; no saturation unknown) and `macos::policed_class_has_its_own_policer_verdict` (a policed `udp/high-port` saturation detected on its PLCY entry, the classes without a saturation unknown). Ground-truth recovery of a per-class policer (REQ-DRV-013) is not yet run (no EXP run in this change).

- **netem** (`export.netem/1`): `delay <base>ms <jitter p50 spread>ms distribution` + `loss gemodel p r 1-h 1-k` (netem's 1-h and 1-k are loss probabilities, so the stored `ge.h` and `ge.k` are written there as they are) + `rate <summary rate>` + `limit <buffer packets>` per direction. A static summary only, as netem cannot replay time-varying capacity (R-0005).
- **Mahimahi** (`export.mahimahi/2`): delivery opportunities converted to ms timestamps, one line per 1504-byte quantum (per-byte accounting: an opportunity of n bytes emits n/1504 lines, carrying the remainder) (R-0001). Line 0 is the first opportunity; the file ends at the last one. The measured opportunities are those of SERS `capacity_opps[dir]`, which lie inside the `saturated` segments of `PATH.<dir>.capacity`. Each gap between two consecutive saturated segments (capacity unknown there, not zero) is filled with synthetic 1504-byte opportunities at `PATH.<dir>.capacity.median_rate_bps`, one every 1504·8/rate s from the end of the earlier segment to the start of the later one, both ends excluded, so that a replay sees the median rate rather than a blackout. A sidecar `<prefix>.<up|down>.json` says so: `method`, `dir`, `quantum_bytes`, `t0_ns` (trace time of ms 0), `lines`, `measured_lines`, `filled_lines`, `fill_rate_bps` and `filled` (one `{start_ms, end_ms}` per filled gap). Without segments or a positive median rate nothing is filled; the sidecar then has `fill_rate_bps: null`, an empty `filled` list and a `reason`. `export.mahimahi/1` did not fill, so gaps replayed as zero capacity, and wrote no sidecar.
- **Mahimahi import** (`import.mahimahi/1`): lines become capacity opportunities with `quantum_bytes = 1504`, `quality = saturated`, `workload = "imported"`. Every other field is `unknown`.
